Why it asks for your password

A MacBook normally goes to sleep the moment you close the lid. Only an administrator can change that, so your Mac asks for your password before Overnight can keep it awake.

The permission lasts one night. When the night is over, or you quit Overnight, your Mac goes back to its usual settings and nothing stays behind. The next night, it asks again.

On a work Mac, ask your IT team first. Some don't allow this.

What Overnight never does

  • Delete your files.
  • Send what you're working on anywhere. Its alerts only say what happened.
  • Keep your Mac awake when the battery is nearly empty or the Mac is too warm.
  • Leave special permissions in place after the night ends.

While you sleep

11 PM

Overnight keeps an eye on your Mac all night. It lets the Mac go to sleep as soon as one of these happens.

  • Claude is done

    Claude has had nothing to do for 15 minutes. It's finished, or it's waiting for you.

  • The battery runs low

    Below 10%, your Mac sleeps to save what's left. Plug it in for a long night.

  • The Mac gets too warm

    If macOS says it's running hot, it sleeps to cool down. Leave it somewhere airy, not in a bag.

  • Morning comes

    At 8:00 AM by default. You can pick another time or turn it off.

Overnight tells you when this happens. If you'd rather keep the Mac awake all night, whatever Claude is doing, click the moon and choose All night instead of While Claude works.

Get a message on your phone

Overnight can send its alerts to your phone through ntfy, a free app for iPhone and Android.

  1. Install the ntfy app from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. Click the moon on your Mac and turn on Send alerts to my phone.
  3. Press Copy topic name. In the ntfy app, tap + and paste it.
  4. Press Send a test alert. Your phone should buzz.

Alerts only say what happened, such as Claude going idle or the battery running low. They never say what Claude was working on. Anyone who knows your topic name could read them, so keep the random name Overnight picks.

Alerts only go to your phone while you're away: when the lid is closed, or nobody has used the Mac for 5 minutes.

If Claude hits its usage limit

2 AM

Claude has usage limits. When it reaches one, it pauses until the limit resets, sometimes in the middle of the night.

Claude can carry on by itself when the limit resets, but only if your Mac is awake. So Overnight keeps it awake until then, even though Claude looks idle while it waits.

Make sure Claude continues by itself

In the Claude app, when the usage-limit message appears, tick Auto-continue when limits reset. If you use Claude Code in Terminal, Overnight shows a short message you can copy into Claude so it checks this for you.

If Claude still hasn't continued 10 minutes after the reset, Overnight lets you know. In the morning it shows which sessions need you: open each one and type "continue".

In the morning

8 AM

Click the moon to see how the night went: how long Claude worked, how long your Mac stayed awake and how much battery it used.

If a session stopped and needs you, Overnight says which one and what to do. It also warns you when your disk is getting full, and can ask Claude to tidy up while you watch.

Install

Day

  1. Download Overnight. It's free.
  2. Open the zip in your Downloads folder, then drag Overnight into Applications.
  3. Open Overnight from Applications.

The latest version needs macOS 12 or later and works on any Mac, with Apple silicon or Intel.

If your Mac won't open it

The first time, your Mac may say it can't check Overnight for malicious software. That's because Overnight isn't registered with Apple yet. You only need to do this once:

  1. Open System Settings and click Privacy & Security.
  2. Scroll down and click Open Anyway next to Overnight.

To have Overnight start when you log in, add it in System Settings, under General, then Login Items. New versions appear on the Releases page. To update, quit Overnight, download it again and replace the old one.

Questions

Which Claude does it work with?

Claude Code, in the Code tab of the Claude desktop app or in Terminal.

How do I stop it?

Click the moon and press Let it sleep. Quitting Overnight works too, and your Mac goes back to normal straight away.

Does it use much battery?

Overnight itself uses almost nothing. Keeping a Mac awake does use battery, so plug it in for a long night. On battery, Overnight lets the Mac sleep at 10%.
